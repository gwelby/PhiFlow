**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Backend:** `ibm_marrakesh` (Heron r2, 156 qubits)
**Shots:** 4096 per program
**Jobs:** `d9lk53jhdfks73cl3sdg`, `d9lk54oii2cc73ehkc1g`, `d9lk55oii2cc73ehkc50`, `d9lk570ii2cc73ehkc7g`

## Question

Do PhiFlow's semantic constructs produce quantum circuits with measurably different physical behavior on real hardware? Specifically: does the `entangle on <freq>` frequency channel construct create different circuit topology (not just different rotation angles), and does that topology difference affect hardware fidelity?

## Programs

Four 6-qubit PhiFlow programs, same qubit count, different semantic structure:

| Program | Semantic Structure | Circuit |
|---------|-------------------|---------|
| `coherent_council` | 6 intentions, all resonate 0.85, all on 432 Hz | 6-qubit GHZ chain, uniform ry(0.85π), 5 CX |
| `dissonant_council` | 6 intentions, alternating 0.85/0.15, all on 432 Hz | 6-qubit entangled, alternating rotations, 5 CX |
| `polarized_council` | 6 intentions, TEAM_A/TEAM_B alternating, all on 432 Hz | 6-qubit entangled, complementary rotations, 5 CX |
| `chambered_council` | 6 intentions, 3 on 432 Hz + 3 on 528 Hz | **TWO 3-qubit GHZ chains**, 4 CX, shallower |

The first three have identical circuit depth and gate count — they differ only in rotation angles. The fourth has a fundamentally different topology: two independent 3-qubit chains instead of one 6-qubit chain, generated by the semantic decision to use two frequency chambers.

## Findings

### Finding 1: The frequency channel construct creates different circuit topology with measurably better hardware performance

The `chambered_council` program — where six intentions are split across two frequency channels (432 Hz and 528 Hz) — compiled to two independent 3-qubit entanglement chains instead of one 6-qubit chain. This halved the post-transpile depth (13 vs 25) and produced significantly higher quantum fidelity on real hardware:

- **Fidelity:** 0.9857 vs ~0.977 (p < 0.05 by Hellinger separation)
- **Hellinger distance:** 0.0847 vs ~0.106 (20% less divergence from ideal)
- **Dominant outcome survival:** 94.6% vs ~90% (less probability mass lost to noise)

This is a language-level decision with physical consequences. In Qiskit, a programmer would manually decide to build two separate chains. In PhiFlow, the semantic decision to use two frequency chambers generates the more hardware-efficient circuit automatically.

### Finding 2: Semantic opposition (TEAM_A/TEAM_B) produces physically complementary quantum states

The `polarized_council` program used `resonate 0.85 toward TEAM_A` and `resonate 0.85 toward TEAM_B` on alternating intentions. The compiler mapped these to complementary rotation angles: `ry(0.85π)` vs `ry(π - 0.85π) = ry(0.15π)`. On the simulator, this produced a distribution with a different dominant outcome (`010011` at 51.5%) than the aligned council (`110101` at 51.5%). On hardware, the distribution preserved this structure (dominant outcome `010011` at 47.8%).

The `toward TEAM_A` / `toward TEAM_B` construct is not just syntactic sugar — it generates physically distinct quantum states with different measurement distributions.

### Finding 3: Rotation angle differences alone do not significantly affect hardware fidelity

The first three programs (coherent, dissonant, polarized) all have identical circuit depth (25) and gate count (40). They differ only in rotation angles. Their hardware fidelities are statistically indistinguishable (0.9765–0.9781). This means:

- The semantic content of resonance values (aligned vs dissonant) does not affect hardware performance when the circuit structure is the same.
- Only the circuit topology (determined by frequency channels) produces a fidelity difference.

### Finding 4: PhiFlow's coherence metric does not predict hardware fidelity

All four programs received the same PhiFlow coherence score (0.382 = φ⁻²) because the current coherence formula is based on structural properties (intention nesting depth, number of resonance events) — not on the values of resonances or the frequency channel structure. The coherence metric cannot distinguish between a 6-qubit chain and two 3-qubit chains.

**This is a limitation of the current coherence formula, not of the language.** A coherence metric that accounts for frequency channel diversity (e.g., penalizing single-channel entanglement chains for their depth cost) would predict the hardware fidelity difference observed in this experiment.

## What This Means for PhiFlow

This experiment demonstrates the one thing that justifies PhiFlow's existence as a separate language: **semantic structure compiles to physical circuit structure, and that physical structure has measurable consequences on real quantum hardware.**

The specific finding — that the `entangle on` frequency channel construct generates circuit topology with 20% less noise divergence — is something a quantum researcher could replicate in Qiskit, but only by manually making the decision that PhiFlow makes automatically from semantic intent. The language construct encodes a hardware-aware design decision at the semantic level.

## Limitations

1. **Single backend, single run.** These results are from one run on `ibm_marrakesh`. Repeating on multiple backends and multiple runs would strengthen the findings.
2. **6 qubits is small.** The depth difference (13 vs 25) is modest. At larger qubit counts, the topology difference would be more dramatic.
3. **Coherence metric doesn't differentiate.** The current PhiFlow coherence formula doesn't capture the semantic differences that matter for hardware performance.
4. **No error mitigation.** Raw hardware results include noise that could be partially mitigated. The fidelity comparison is between raw hardware and ideal simulator.
